Studio principles

Software should serve the owner.

Docklight Labs products are built around control, durability, and direct usefulness.

Offline firstCore functions should keep working without a connection. Networking should enhance an app, unless the job itself genuinely needs the internet.
Bought onceSoftware: buy once, own it. No forced subscription to keep using what you bought. Ongoing services are different — a service can have a subscription.
Private by defaultNo unnecessary logins, tracking, or cloud storage. If the function genuinely requires them, we explain why. We hate that stuff too. We don’t want to be those people.
Focused scopeOne clear job beats a sprawling feature list.
“Ship what you can own. That’s the lane.”
Who we are

Built from practical experience.

I’m Darrell Batton. Most people call me db. DockLight Labs is my software studio, and Florida Hillbilly is part of the same family. Different projects, same guy.

I started with a TRS-80 and a cassette recorder. Then came the original Macintosh at school, the Commodore 64 and 128, the Amiga, and the first PCs. I bought a $4,000 XT that was obsolete in less than a year. An expensive education.

In the military, I worked with mainframes and alphabet soup like NSA and JCL. Paper tape, punch cards, 9-track tapes, removable disk platters bigger than wedding cakes, and 8-inch floppies. Eventually somebody introduced me to this strange new thing called a CD.

Computers have been there for almost as long as I can remember. The machines change. Figuring out how the pieces fit together — and making them useful — is still the job.
